Why Should You Check Broken Links?

Broken links can frustrate visitors and make it harder for search engines to discover your website content. Regularly checking your links helps maintain a better user experience and a healthier website.

How to Use the Broken Link Checker

  1. Enter your website URL, including https://.
  2. Select how deeply you want the tool to crawl your website.
  3. Click the Check Broken Links button.
  4. Review the results and filter links by status or type.
  5. Export the report as a CSV file for further analysis.

Understanding Link Statuses

  • Working: The link returned a successful response.
  • Broken: The destination returned a confirmed 404 or 410 response.
  • Redirect: The destination redirected to another URL.
  • Other errors: Access restrictions, server errors, timeouts or network problems. These are not automatically classified as broken.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a broken link?

A broken link points to a destination that cannot be accessed successfully, often because the page has been removed or the URL is incorrect.

Is this Broken Link Checker free?

Yes. You can use this tool for free without purchasing a subscription.

Can I check external links?

Yes. The tool checks both internal links and external links found on the scanned pages.

Does the tool check an entire website?

The current scan is limited to a maximum of 20 internal pages and 100 unique links. It does not guarantee that every page on a large website will be checked.

What does HTTP 404 mean?

HTTP 404 means the requested resource was not found. HTTP 410 means the resource is gone.

Are redirects considered broken links?

No. Redirects are reported separately because they may lead visitors to a valid destination.

Can I download the scan report?

Yes. After a scan is complete, use Export CSV to download the link report.

Why might some links show other errors?

Some websites block automated requests or temporarily fail to respond. Access restrictions and timeouts are reported separately rather than being treated as confirmed broken links.

Note: Scan results depend on the responses returned by destination websites at the time of checking. Some websites may restrict automated requests, so results should be reviewed before making changes.
